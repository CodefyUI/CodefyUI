# Train a VLA on PushWorld

Behavior-clone a small vision-language-action policy end to end, evaluate it
closed-loop, and watch the rollout video the run writes. Everything is a
first-class node; the graph is the whole method.

**What you need:** a CUDA GPU (measured on an RTX 4080: about 56 minutes and
under 3 GB of VRAM) and nothing else — no dataset download, no simulator
install. The environment, the demonstrations, and the videos are all
generated by the graph.

## What the graph does

1. `PushWorldEnv` — a language-conditioned 2D push benchmark: a white agent
   disc, colored pucks, colored ring targets, and an instruction like
   "push the red puck to the blue target". With one distractor puck and two
   targets, pixels alone cannot identify the goal — the policy must read
   the instruction.
2. `PushWorldDemos` — 2,400 scripted-expert episodes become ~62k
   behavior-cloning samples, plus a 150-episode held-out split from a
   disjoint seed stream. `demo_noise 0.5` is DART-style: the EXECUTED
   action is perturbed while the expert's stays the label, which is the
   recovery data closed-loop control needs (measured: 4% closed-loop
   success without it, at an otherwise identical budget).
3. `VLAModel` — 3.3M parameters: a three-conv stem + byte-level instruction
   embedding feed a 4-layer trunk, and a 2-layer action expert predicts
   8-step action chunks (`head_type: regression`; flip to `flow_matching`
   for the pi0/SmolVLA-style velocity-field head on identical data). The
   node emits its own mode-matched `loss_fn`, so a mismatched loss cannot
   be wired.
4. `TrainingLoop` — 110 epochs (~26,840 optimizer steps) with AdamW 1e-3
   and `warmup_cosine` stepped per OPTIMIZER step (`scheduler_step:
   optimizer_step`, `total_steps: 26840` — the advisory stays silent
   because the schedule fits the run).
5. `VLARollout` — 30 fresh episodes, receding horizon `execute_k: 2`
   (predict 8, execute 2, re-plan). `VLAActionEval` — open-loop chunk MSE
   on the held-out split.
6. Two `VideoWrite` nodes — the expert demonstrations and the policy's own
   rollouts (green/red border per outcome) land under `/api/media/vla/` and
   play inline in the editor.

## Measured reference numbers (RTX 4080, this exact graph)

| metric | value |
| --- | --- |
| train loss | 0.770 -> 0.0175 |
| closed-loop success_rate | **0.967** (29/30) |
| avg episode length | 45.6 steps of 120 |
| open-loop action MSE (holdout) | 0.588 |
| swapped-instruction success | **0.033** (1/30) |

The last row is the point of the example. Set `VLARollout.instruction_mode:
swapped` (it hands the policy a deliberately wrong puck color) and success
collapses from 0.967 to 0.033 while the weights are provably identical —
the policy demonstrably acts on the language, not just the pixels. To rerun
only the evaluation after training once: leave the graph alone, set
`TrainingLoop.epochs: 1` and `max_steps: 1` (weights persist between runs;
one warmup-floor step changes nothing measurable), flip the mode, and run.

## Knobs worth studying

- `execute_k` (VLARollout): 2 -> 0.86-0.97, 4 -> ~0.7, 8 (full chunk,
  open loop) -> ~0.4 on one trained policy — the compounding-error curve
  as a single parameter.
- `head_type` (VLAModel): regression vs flow_matching on identical data.
- `vision_stem` (VLAModel, advanced): `conv` vs the classic ViT `patchify`
  — the "early convolutions help transformers see" effect is the
  difference between this policy and a ~2x worse one.
- `demo_noise` (PushWorldDemos): 0 turns DART off and closed-loop success
  collapses; the param text quotes the measurement.
- Wire the study through `run_graph_experiments` /
  `optimize_graph_parameters` (Graph Copilot) to vary exactly one of these
  per variant with success_rate as the ranked metric.

## Scaling down

For a quick smoke on weaker hardware: `episodes: 600`, `epochs: 30`,
`LRScheduler.total_steps: 3660` trains in a few minutes but lands well
short of the reference success rate — the full budget is what earns 0.97.
